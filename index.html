The Takeaway:

With 100 doors, picking the car on your first attempt is extremely unlikely (1%). When the host opens 98 doors containing goats, they intentionally filter out almost all bad choices. All that probability (99%) is now concentrated in the one remaining unopened door!

Mathematical Explanation

1

The Initial Pick

When you pick a door at the start, there is a 1 in 3 chance (33.3%) that you picked the car, and a 2 in 3 chance (66.7%) that the car is behind one of the other two doors.

2

The Host's Constraint

Monty Hall knows where the car is. He must always reveal a goat from the unchosen doors. He is not choosing randomly; his knowledge forces him to keep the car safe if you picked a goat.

3

Probability Transfer

Revealing a goat doesn't alter your initial 1/3 chance of being right. Instead, it shifts all 2/3 probability of the other two doors into the single remaining unopened door!
